# UX QA methods and research literacy

Select a method from the PRD task and intended result. A product test proves scoped behavior, not what a population notices or understands.

## Pick the method by decision

| Decision or uncertainty | Method and how to apply it | What it can establish / what it cannot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What actually happens in the user's setting? | Contextual inquiry and task analysis: observe the task in situ with consent; record inputs, handoffs, workarounds, disruptions, device and consequences. Contrast observation with what was said. Turn service handoffs into explicit starting states and recovery cases. | Actual context for observed participants; not how common the pattern is. A persona can propose a context, not validate it. |
| Are there obvious interaction defects? | Heuristic inspection: reviewers independently follow the same scoped tasks. Record the visible state and violated principle (status, user control, consistency, error prevention or recovery); reconcile duplicate findings after review. | Candidate issues and observed defects, not representative incidence or independent evidence when multiple models use the same trace. |
| Can a newcomer discover, interpret and execute the next step? | Cognitive walkthrough: for each intended step, ask whether the goal occurs to the user, the action is noticed, the label connects to the goal, and feedback shows progress. Record the first broken link and alternate path. | A learnability hypothesis; not measured user behavior. Use behavior guidance and compare with a task-based observation. |
| Is the problem information structure or visual presentation? | Card sort to generate grouping/label hypotheses; tree test with neutral tasks to test first choice and findability; then compare the same task in the real UI to see effects of placement, layout and controls. | Tree-only results do not evaluate rendered UI, search or motor effort. Follow the information-architecture reference for required disclosure. |
| Can people actually complete or recover? | Moderated usability test with relevant participants, neutral tasks and predeclared unassisted success, assisted success and failure. For benchmarking, standardize tasks and conditions, record denominator and time rules, and keep failures distinct from technical exclusions. | Direct observed strategies and failures; a small purposive round finds problems, not population prevalence. Think-aloud or prompts change timing and must not be mixed into an unassisted speed benchmark. |
| Is an action physically awkward or visually hard to find? | Human factors: inspect relevant posture, grip, hand, device, input and effective hit region. Use Fitts-style target/distance comparisons for matched pointing conditions and existing ergonomics checks for reach and occlusion. Validate on target devices with people when the decision needs real reach, fatigue or viewing behavior. | Relative hypotheses or measured layout facts under stated conditions; no universal tap-time, comfort or thumb-reach claim from CSS geometry or synthetic body parameters. |
| Can the same complete task be performed using assistive input? | Accessibility evaluation: scope the platform, version and applicable criteria; sample representative views and complete processes; combine automated checks with manual keyboard, focus, zoom/reflow and screen-reader checks as relevant. Reference WCAG-EM for structure and KWCAG/platform guidance where applicable. | Observed criterion results for the evaluated scope only. A scanner, an accessibility tree, or a synthetic persona alone cannot certify conformance. |
| Did a change improve outcomes for users? | Start with the user goal, observable signal and metric (HEART). Prefer comparable baselines; for causal claims, use a properly authorized randomized study with declared units, exposure, guardrails, sample plan and decision rule. Inspect allocation and missing events before interpreting effects. | Before/after can show an association but cannot rule out confounding. Repeated personas, seeds or retries are not independent participants and cannot produce human-population confidence intervals. |
| Did a fix survive interruption and repeated use? | Browser/device regression: drive the actual UI on a pinned build, set up success, failure, cancellation, retry and reopen states, and verify the final outcome independently (not just a toast). Use fixtures for unsafe actions. | Repeatable product behavior in the named environment only. An adapter or contract test is useful but not the same as user-visible replay. |

## Apply the methods without changing the evidence contract

1. Write the question, PRD outcome, target user context and critical error (e.g. loss, wrong account, duplicate action). Name the minimum method that answers it and the condition that calls for a stronger one. Select input, lifecycle, device and data strata because they can change the outcome, not to inflate a count.
2. Before execution, pin task wording, build, initial state, device/input, allowed assistance, observable completion, time window and stopping rule. For human research, record recruitment rationale, consent and limits; do not treat demographics as proxies for measured abilities. For synthetic work, record profile source, modeled inputs and which behaviors were not observed.
3. Record observations, interpretation and recommendation separately. Use the existing scenario and evidence-bundle contract. For each material finding capture the steps, visible or measured mismatch, consequence, evidence locator and coverage, then name the missing evidence. Keep cases that are untested or inconclusive separate from failure and success. Separate severity (harm, reversibility, blocked user goal) from frequency; do not infer frequency from severity, personas, or the number of repeated runs.
4. Triangulate with evidence that can disagree: e.g., an independent persisted-state check adjudicates a screen claim, and a no-hints participant test probes the learnability claim. Keep conflicting observations tied to their conditions instead of averaging them. Preserve the UI's design language unless the evidence shows the design itself is blocking the task.
5. Verify both the causal mechanism of the defect and the protected user outcome after a fix. Preserve evidence and existing rollback boundaries.

Example: a data-import preview says “saved,” but the list is empty after reopening. A controlled failure and reopen test can establish the mismatch on that build. A walkthrough identifies which status cue misleads; a moderated task tests whether people understand the recovery path. Neither a cluster of simulated personas nor a clickthrough rate establishes the real-user impact.

## Convenience: first use, repeat use and recovery

Can the user reach the **right result** with less avoidable work? For example, compare the same delivery-address task on a disposable checkout: first enter a new address; on a return visit select the intended saved address, or enter it if saving is not offered. Use two plausible addresses so a wrong default can actually fail. Verify the destination on the review screen without placing an order. Compare first use **before vs after** and repeat use **before vs after**, not a newcomer's first attempt against a practiced user's fastest one. Match task, initial data, device/input and assistance within each comparison.

- Start the clock at the task prompt; stop at the independently verified correct result. Report unassisted correct / all attempts, wrong outcomes (including apparent success), abandonment and assistance separately. Time successful attempts separately from time spent before failure. Count actual re-entry of valid data, backtracking and corrections; a click saved while selecting the wrong address is not a win.
- Interrupt at the same step (e.g. Back from review), then resume. Check retained edits, focus and the selected address with the relevant input, including keyboard or screen reader when in scope. On the web, [WCAG 2.2 SC 3.3.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/redundant-entry.html) calls for auto-population or a way to select previously supplied information **within the same process**, except when re-entry is essential, needed for security or the value is no longer valid. Browser autofill alone is insufficient. This does not require storing addresses across visits.
- Keep consent, security and consequential-action review intact. Record whether a safeguard caught an error rather than counting it as wasted effort. Scripted replay can establish that a route works, not that people find, prefer or trust it. Observe people without hints before claiming a shortcut is discoverable.

[Seckler et al., CHI 2014](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/pdf/42513.pdf) compared bundled changes to three registration forms: completion time improved significantly for two, not the third. That result motivates testing the actual task; it does not predict the gain from any one edit. [GOV.UK's benchmarking protocol](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/usability-benchmarking-a-website-or-whole-service#what-to-measure) distinguishes correct completion from giving up or believing a failed attempt succeeded.

### Synthetic evidence card: returning to the saved list

In an owned fixture, review saved item `Q-17` at revision `B` without changing the list. Add a lookalike `Q-18`. Interrupt at the saved-list checkpoint just before opening the item. Replay from that same list and checkpoint on the same build, input, viewport, interruption and observation limit. When comparing builds, vary only the build. The fixture offers `Not now` as a valid exit; verify it leaves the list unchanged. For completion, verify the intended item and revision in the review result and confirm the list is unchanged; a click or banner is not enough.

Start one elapsed-time clock at the first observation of the reopened list being actionable. Record what was visible and tie the start and subsequent events to the same run. The launcher click, loading state and no-op inputs do not count as resumption. Record the first task-relevant move and any wrong turns, the first verified action that continues the intended task, and the final outcome separately. Judge actions by the recorded state, not by which route the reviewer expected or by guessing intent.

| Invented trace from the same checkpoint | Record separately |
| --- | --- |
| At 2 s opens `Q-18`; at 6 s reaches `Q-17/B`; at 10 s verifies the review and unchanged list. | Wrong first move retained; correct continuation at 6 s; completion verified at 10 s. |
| At 2 s opens `Q-17/B`; at 10 s verifies the review and unchanged list. | Correct first move and continuation at 2 s; completion verified at 10 s. |
| At 2 s chooses `Not now`; independent readback shows the list unchanged. | Valid decline, not a wrong turn, continuation or task completion. |
| The trace is continuous through the 20 s observation limit, with no correct continuation or verified completion. | Report no event observed through 20 s (right-censored), not a 20 s success latency or a known ultimate failure. |
| The actionable return point or required event evidence is missing. | The affected timing or outcome is unavailable; keep any independently observed result and do not substitute 0 s or the observation limit. |

[Trafton et al. (2003), §4.1.4, Measures](https://interruptions.net/literature/Trafton-IJHCS03.pdf#page=10) measured from restoration of the task interface to the first input to resume it; that is not this card's verified continuation. [Altmann et al. (2014), Analysis 1](https://interruptions.net/literature/Altmann-JExpPsycholGen14.pdf#page=4) separated step-order errors from response latency. These lab tasks motivate separate measures, not a product timing target or a claim about users. The times above are invented for the evidence card.
